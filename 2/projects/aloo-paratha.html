Aloo Parathas

Dough

  • 240gms atta (whole wheat flour)
  • 1.25-1.5 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp oil/ghee

Stuffing

  • 4 medium sized potatoes, boiled and peeled
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • 1 tsp garam masala
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp chili powder
  • 1 tsp dry mango powder (amchur)
  • 2 tbsp coriander leaves, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp red onions, finely chopped
  • 2 green chilies, finely chopped

Frying

  • 6 tbsp oil/ghee (1 tbsp per paratha)

makes roughly 6 parathas

Stuffing

  1. Boil (or microwave) the potatoes with the peel on. Pat them dry before peeling them.
  2. Mash /grate them till no chunks remain. Let it cool completely before adding the remaining ingredients in the stuffing section.
  3. Mix well and set it aside.
  4. The stuffing should be roughly the same consistency as the dough: if it's too hard or too soft it can tear through the dough.

Dough

  1. Combine wheat flour and salt and make a well.
  2. Add a tablespoon of oil with 1 cup of water and start kneading.
  3. Add remaining water in small increments and continue to knead till the dough comes together as a ball.
  4. Add the remaining oil to the dough and knead till the oil is absorbed.
  5. If the dough is sticky, sprinkle approximately 1/4 teaspoon wheat flour (or more if needed) on the dough and knead again to form a dough that is not sticky. The dough should be supple and soft.
  6. If the dough isn’t soft enough (roughly as soft as the filling), knead some oil (a little at a time) into the dough until it reaches the desired texture.
  7. Cover the dough and set aside for 15-20 minutes.

Rolling out the parathas

  1. Divide dough into 6 equal portions (~70g) and roll into balls. Flatten the ball and cover both sides with flour.
  2. Roll into a circular disc about 4 inches in diameter.
  3. Place an equal amount (by volume) of the stuffing in the center (~60g).
  4. Grab the edges and pleat them and bring them together in the center.
  5. Pinch them together to seal them and flatten them using your palm.
  6. Flatten the ball and cover both sides with flour.
  7. Use your rolling pin to roll into a flat, round shape. Do this delicately - dusting flour as needed to help with this process. Don't worry about the filling breaking through if it does, but make sure to keep your rolling pin well-floured and scrape off any stuffing that clings to it so it doesn't affect future parathas.

Frying the parathas

  1. Place a tawa/frying pan on gas on high heat.
  2. Once heated, carefully place the paratha on the tawa. Cook until it appears dry on the top. Turn it over and cook the other side.
  3. Flip the paratha and add about 1/2 a tablespoon of ghee/oil on the paratha.
  4. Cook the side for about 15 seconds. Repeat the process with the other side. Use a spatula to press the edges to ensure the sides are cooked through.
  5. Serve hot with butter, yogurt, achaar, and maybe a fried egg.
